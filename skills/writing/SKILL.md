# Writing

Write text without AI patterns, or edit text to remove them. Keep the meaning and the intended tone.

## Any language

Each rule describes what a phrase does, not which words it uses. Find the same pattern in whatever language you write.

- Write in the reader's language. Keep code, identifiers, commands and established terms as they are.
- The language's own norms win: punctuation, quotes, capitalization, word order.
- Write what a native speaker would write, not a translation from English.

## Patterns to detect and fix

### Content

- **Tacked-on participle phrases.** "..., highlighting the importance of", "..., ensuring that". Delete, or make it a real claim with a source.
- **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe". Name the source or delete.

### Language

- **Inflated vocabulary.** Words that sound important but say nothing: crucial, delve, seamless, showcase, landscape. Use the plain word or cut.
- **Fancy ways to say "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts". Write "is" or "has".
- **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
- **Rule of three.** Use the natural number of items, not three by default.
- **Synonym cycling.** Pick one word for a thing and repeat it.
- **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y aren't on one scale. List the items.

### Style

- **Dash overuse.** Use a period or a comma. Keep a dash only where grammar requires it, and never write a hyphen in its place.
- **Colon overuse.** A colon goes before a list or an example, not between two clauses.
- **Boldface overuse.** Don't bold every name or acronym.
- **Inline-header lists.** "**Performance:** Performance improved..." restates itself. Turn it into prose. A bold lead-in ending in a period, followed by new detail, is fine.
- **Capitalized headings.** Use sentence case or the language's own rules.
- **Decorative emojis.** Remove from headings and bullets.
- **Quotes.** One quote style in prose, straight quotes in code.

### Replies

- **Answer first.** Then details. Don't restate the question, don't end with a summary that repeats the answer.
- **No structure in short replies.** A short reply has no headings. Use a list only for separate items.

### Communication artifacts

- **Chatbot phrases.** "I hope this helps!", "Certainly!". Remove.
- **Sycophantic tone.** "Great question!" Respond directly.

### Filler

- **Filler phrases.** "in order to" is "to", "due to the fact that" is "because". Delete "it is important to note that".
- **Excessive hedging.** One hedge at most.
- **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State a specific plan or fact.

### Jargon

- **Abstract metaphor nouns.** substrate, vector, paradigm, surface, scaffolding, north star, ecosystem. Use the concrete word.

### Plain speech

- **Say what it does, not how it feels.** Name the mechanism or a number. If a sentence could appear unchanged in another project, cut it.
- **Dense sentences.** One idea per sentence. If the reader has to reread it, split it.
- **Active voice.** Name who acts: "the compiler validates queries". Passive only when the actor doesn't matter.
- **Adverbs.** Use a stronger verb or a number instead.
- **Plain words.** "use" over "utilize", "check" over "perform a check".
- **Mannered prose.** No aphorisms, personified code or figurative verbs. Say it literally.
- **Over-compression.** No arrows, symbol-speak or dropped words. Write whole sentences.
- **Calques from English.** In other languages, don't translate English idioms or word order literally. Write as a native speaker would.
